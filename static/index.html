What this can and cannot do

Read this before trading off the panel
  • The strong result is on volatility, not direction. Dealer gamma relates well to forward realised vol and to mean-reversion versus trend character. Its relationship to forward returns is weak.
  • Direction comes from the drift layer. Charm and vanna are genuinely directional, but they are a two-week flow with a known expiry date, not a thesis.
  • The dealer positioning assumption is the largest error source. Everything here assumes dealers are long calls and short puts. That is a heuristic, not a measurement, and every number downstream inherits it.
  • Levels are only as good as the open interest behind them, and open interest changes intraday. A stale map is worse than no map because it is confidently wrong.
  • These are probability tilts, not signals. Realistically this moves a 50/50 to roughly 57/43 on the volatility regime, and less on direction. Position sizing and invalidation levels do more work than the analysis does.
  • 0DTE has changed the picture. A large share of SPX gamma is born and dies within a session, so the static profile is less durable than it was before 2022.
  • The auction profile is regular-hours SPY. No free intraday ES source exists, so the overnight auction is missing.

What this can and cannot do

Read this before trading off the panel
  • The composite is a summary, not a signal. Five loosely-correlated sentiment proxies blended into one number is navigation. The sub-score breakdown and the divergences between them are the actual output.
  • Sentiment analysis here is extractive. VADER with a finance and WSB lexicon measures the tone of headlines and posts, not whether the news is good for the equity. A takeover premium and a short-seller report can both read positive.
  • Dealer gamma is about volatility, not direction. Positive gamma damps moves and negative gamma amplifies them; neither is bullish. Direction here comes from the flip level, wall structure and open-interest gravity, and gamma only flags amplification risk.
  • Short interest is fuel, not a direction — which is why it is not in the composite at all. A heavily shorted name that is rising has squeeze potential; the same short interest on a falling name means the shorts are winning. It is scored as potential in its own panel, and reaches the composite only as the squeeze-fuel divergence.
  • StockTwits skews bullish at baseline. A 70% bull ratio is close to its resting state. Velocity against this ticker's own history is the signal, and that needs days of stored snapshots before it means anything.
  • Retail sentiment is positioning, not insight. It is most useful as a contrarian input at extremes and when it conflicts with what insiders and the options market are doing.
  • Finviz is scraped, not an API. A layout change breaks short interest, ownership and analyst data at once. The panel degrades to a stale badge rather than guessing.
  • The option chain is ~15 minutes delayed and the dealer convention (long calls, short puts) is a rougher proxy for a single name than it is for an index.

How to read these panels

Two sentiment numbers, and why they disagree
  • TONE is the language; ASSET READ is the direction. Tone is VADER with a macro lexicon — how positive the words are. Asset read is a rule layer that maps each headline to a direction for the asset this panel covers. "Gold rises on weaker dollar as rate-cut bets firm" is negative language describing a bullish setup, and the two numbers say so.
  • The disagreement is the point. When tone and direction line up, the panel is telling you what you already saw in the price. When they diverge, the coverage is framing a move in the opposite emotional register — that is flagged explicitly.
  • Check coverage before trusting the direction. Most headlines are not directional at all, and only those that fire a rule are scored. A reading drawn from 2 of 15 headlines is two headlines, not a signal, and is labelled thin.
  • Signs are per panel, not universal. A rise in yields is bearish in US Fixed Income; a rise in crude is bullish in Oil & Energy. The same verb means opposite things in different panels, which is exactly why one global sentiment score would be useless here.
  • It is pattern matching, not comprehension. No negation handling ("yields fail to rise"), no distinction between a forecast and a fact, and a headline about two assets moving opposite ways is resolved by word proximity rather than grammar.
  • Some prints are genuinely two-sided. A hot payrolls number is good for growth and bad for rate cuts. Those are scored at half weight and flagged rather than forced to pick a side.
  • 48 hours, newest 25 per panel. Undated items are dropped rather than assumed recent. On a quiet weekend the niche panels go thin, and say so.
  • Google's when: filter is not honoured on keyword queries — it returned eleven-year-old articles during testing. Recency here comes from a hard timestamp window applied locally, never from the feed's own filtering.
  • Market levels are daily closes, not live quotes, and futures and cash indices keep different holiday calendars — so a strip row can lag its panel by a session.
